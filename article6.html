CANDU Reactors

Explore Canadian-designed heavy water reactors and their virtual reality applications.

Understanding CANDU Reactors

CANDU Reactors are Canadian-designed heavy water reactors that represent a pinnacle of nuclear engineering. These reactors are fueled by natural uranium and utilize heavy water (deuterium oxide) as both moderator and coolant.

How CANDU Reactors Work

The process begins with natural uranium converted into fuel pellets. Heat is generated by splitting uranium atoms in a process called nuclear fission. The moderator slows down neutrons to increase the probability of them interacting with nuclei to cause nuclear fission.

The nuclear fission reaction releases heat into the moderator. The heated heavy water is then moved to steam generators where the heat is transferred to regular water and converted into pressurized steam. The pressurized steam pushes the turbine blades and causes the rotor to spin. The rotor is a large magnet producing magnetic fields which move across copper wires to produce electricity.

Key Components

  • Fuel Channels: Contain the uranium fuel bundles
  • Calandria: Large cylindrical vessel containing the moderator
  • End Shields: Provide radiation protection and support
  • Safety Systems: Multiple redundant safety mechanisms
