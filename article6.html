CANDU Reactor Physics

Explore the fundamental physics and engineering principles behind Canadian Deuterium Uranium reactors.

CANDU Reactors

CANDU Reactors are Canadian-designed heavy water reactors that represent a pinnacle of nuclear engineering innovation. These reactors are fueled by natural uranium and utilize heavy water, also known as deuterium oxide, as both moderator and coolant.

Nuclear Fission Process

The natural uranium is converted into fuel pellets, and heat is generated by splitting these uranium atoms in a process called nuclear fission. The moderator acts to slow down neutrons to increase the probability of them interacting with nuclei to cause nuclear fission. This controlled chain reaction is the heart of the CANDU reactor's power generation capability.

Heat Transfer and Power Generation

The nuclear fission reaction releases heat into the moderator. The heated heavy water is then moved to steam generators where the heat is transferred to regular water and converted into pressurized steam. The pressurized steam then pushes the turbine blades and causes the rotor to spin. The rotor is a large magnet producing magnetic fields which move across copper wires and produce electricity.

Key CANDU Components

Understanding the essential components that make CANDU reactors safe and efficient

Fuel Channels

Horizontal pressure tubes containing natural uranium fuel bundles, where the nuclear fission process occurs to generate heat.

Endshields

Protective barriers at each end of the reactor core that provide radiation shielding and support fuel channel connections.

Heavy Water Moderator

Deuterium oxide that slows down neutrons to increase the probability of fission reactions with natural uranium fuel.

Coolant System

Heavy water coolant that removes heat from the fuel channels and transfers it to steam generators for power production.

Control Systems

Sophisticated systems including control rods and shutdown systems that regulate reactor power and ensure safe operation.

Safety Systems

Multiple redundant safety systems designed to shut down the reactor and cool the fuel in emergency situations.

Virtual Reality Model of a CANDU Reactor

Our VR model showcases the CANDU Calandria in unprecedented detail. The VR environment comprises a full-scale model of a CANDU reactor and a variety of simulations to demonstrate its functionality.

Innovative VR Technology

This project incorporates innovative virtual reality technologies into the nuclear field to modernize the education and training process regarding CANDU reactors. Our remotely accessible VR model and simulations provide an interactive and immersive full-scale 3D model of a CANDU reactor.

Interactive Exploration

The VR environment of the 3D CANDU reactor allows users to walk through and explore its mechanisms and functions in ways that would be impossible in real-world scenarios. Students can:

  • Examine fuel channels and their internal structure
  • Observe coolant flow patterns throughout the system
  • Interact with control mechanisms and safety systems
  • View cross-sections and internal components
  • Experience the scale and complexity of the reactor

CANDU Technical Specifications

Reactor Design Features

  • Fuel Type: Natural uranium (no enrichment required)
  • Moderator: Heavy water (D₂O) at low temperature and pressure
  • Coolant: Heavy water (D₂O) under high pressure
  • Reactor Vessel: Calandria vessel containing fuel channels
  • Fuel Handling: On-power refueling capability

Safety Features

  • Passive Safety: Inherent safety characteristics due to physics
  • Multiple Barriers: Fuel cladding, pressure boundary, containment
  • Shutdown Systems: Two independent fast-acting shutdown systems
  • Emergency Cooling: Multiple emergency core cooling systems
  • Containment: Robust containment structure
